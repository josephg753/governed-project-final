# Model card: Lakeshore Readmission Consortium, federated readmitted_30d model

Generated facts (gate, rounds, per-round participation) are in `artifacts/federated_model_card.md`, written by `python run_federation.py`. The member differences, baselines and privacy cost below come from `python experiments/member_differences.py` (`artifacts/experiments/member_differences.md` and `.json`, seed 690, charter v1.1). Every number here can be rebuilt with those two commands.

## Intended use and prohibited uses
Intended: prioritize nurse follow-up calls and discharge planning resources at the home member (Lakeshore General). The prediction ranks discharges; a clinician decides and approves every call or home visit.

Prohibited: coverage decisions, pricing, denying or delaying a discharge, any use by a payer. The agent policy forbids `change_discharge_decision` for any agent, and the coordinator declines pricing and coverage questions.

## Training
Charter v1.1 (`governance/federation_policy.json`): FedAvg (weighted mean), 15 rounds, up to 5 local epochs, all 5 members every round (minimum 3). Each member clips its own update to norm 2.0; secure aggregation is on (the coordinator sees only the masked sum); DP noise multiplier 0.3 on the sum. Gate floors: global accuracy 0.68, every member 0.66, gap 0.08. Gate result: PASS (global 0.694, worst member Riverbend 0.6825, gap 0.0325; `artifacts/federation_gate_report.json`).

Personalization clause (added in v1.1): a member may fine-tune the shared model for 3 epochs on its own rows and use that version for its own patients when it beats the shared model on its neutral slice by 0.02 or more.

## The member difference that changed a conclusion
Question 5 asks whether Harbor should get its own model. The naive reading of the baselines says yes: Harbor's own 700-row model (0.708) beats the shared model (0.695). Experiments E2 and E4 change that conclusion.

1. The difference is real and specific: lives_alone works in the opposite direction at Harbor (E2), because Harbor pairs patients who live alone with a visiting nurse. One shared weight (+0.207) is wrong for Harbor.
2. Leaving Harbor out is worse for everyone (E4): trained without Harbor, the shared model scores Harbor at 0.640 and Riverbend drops from 0.682 to 0.632. Harbor's data helps the consortium.
3. Fine-tuning beats both options (E5): 3 local epochs give Harbor 0.725, above its own model (0.708) and the shared model (0.695).

Decision: no separate model. Charter v1.1 adds the personalization clause instead, and it qualifies St. Brigid (+0.040) and Harbor (+0.030). It also produced a prescriptive rule: at Lakeshore, high-risk patients who live alone, go home and have no follow-up booked are recommended a home visit, since Harbor's program shows that lever works.

Secure aggregation is free. DP 0.3 costs 1.85 points overall, and the cost falls unevenly: Pinecrest (-0.042) and St. Brigid (-0.038) pay most, Lakeshore nothing. DP 1.0 fails the member floor (Riverbend 0.593), so the charter keeps 0.3.

## Limitations and fairness
- Logistic regression on 14 standardized features, synthetic data, teaching only.
- Evaluated per member, not per segment (ward, payer, age band) inside a member. The St. Brigid calibration gap (-0.059) means probabilities there read low; a threshold of 0.5 under-flags St. Brigid patients.
- Pinecrest's slice of the evaluation set is 400 rows, drawn from a 90-row population, so its accuracy estimates are less stable than the others.
- The model is used for scoring only at Lakeshore. Personalized versions for St. Brigid and Harbor are approved by the charter but would be run by those members, not by this agent.
